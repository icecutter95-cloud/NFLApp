================================================================================
NFL + COLLEGE FOOTBALL BETTING MODELS — METHODOLOGY, INPUTS, AND FINDINGS
As of 2026-08-03
================================================================================

PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT
------------------------
This is a self-contained description of two betting models (NFL and college
football) written so an outside reader can critique it without access to the
code. It is deliberately written to expose weaknesses rather than sell results.

A previous outside critique of this project found a real and serious error (a
sign convention bug that had inflated an apparent 70% win rate to nothing), so
the intent here is to invite the same treatment. Sections marked
"OPEN QUESTION" are where critique is most wanted.

Everything below is measured, not asserted. Where a number is uncertain or the
sample is small, that is stated.


================================================================================
PART 1 — THE CENTRAL THESIS
================================================================================

The NFL app rests on a single finding: we cannot beat the closing line at
predicting football, but the weekly OPENING line is softer, and the movement
between open and close is partly predictable.

So the model does not try to answer "who covers?" It answers "which way is this
number going to move?" You take the early price, and closing line value (CLV)
accrues whether or not any individual bet wins.

CLV is the primary metric because it resolves in weeks rather than seasons. Win
rate on ~45 bets a year cannot be distinguished from break-even; it would take
roughly 700 bets (about 15 NFL seasons) to separate 57% from 52.4%.

DEFINITIONS USED THROUGHOUT
  spread convention   NEGATIVE = home favored (standard sportsbook convention)
  week_open           the line at the first snapshot inside 10 days of kickoff
  close               the last snapshot before kickoff
  movement            closing_spread_home - week_open_spread_home
  CLV (points)        home bet: open - close;  away bet: close - open
  break-even          52.38% at -110
  disagreement        predicted_margin + week_open_spread_home
                      (>0 means the model rates home above what the opener implies)


================================================================================
PART 2 — NFL MODEL
================================================================================

2.1 DATA SOURCES
  nfl_data_py       schedules, play-by-play, injuries, rosters (2018-2025)
  The Odds API      line snapshots, DraftKings anchor, historical from mid-2020
  Open-Meteo        historical weather per game
  Supabase          storage, views, live pipeline

  Line coverage limits the usable sample: 1,612 games, 2020-2025.

2.2 MODELS (three, all XGBoost: depth 3, lr 0.03, reg_alpha 1.0, reg_lambda 5.0,
    recency weighted 0.75^seasons_back)

  movement_model        predicts week_spread_movement. Inputs = 57 team features
                        PLUS the opener itself.
  margin_model          predicts home_margin. Inputs = the same 57 features,
                        WITHOUT any line input. Kept separate on purpose (see
                        2.6 "contamination").
  total_movement_model  predicts week_total_movement.

2.3 FEATURES (57)
  16  EPA and success rate, opponent-adjusted, rolling L4/L8, offense+defense
  10  injury aggregates (QB out, offensive/defensive starters out, questionable)
   5  rest days, bye week, short week
   3  travel miles, timezone delta, absolute timezone delta
   2  home field advantage (per team), divisional flag
  21  other: pace, third down, red zone, pressure rate, NGS-derived,
      scoring decomposition, expected turnovers, weather

  Opponent adjustment subtracts the faced unit's established strength, computed
  from PRIOR weeks only.
  In-season metrics are blended with a prior via (n*in_season + k*prior)/(n+k),
  k=3. NOTE: k was fitted before the sign bug was found and has never been
  re-validated on the corrected target.

2.4 VALIDATION PROTOCOL
  train   2020-2022
  select  2023-2024   (thresholds chosen here)
  holdout 2025        (looked at once)
  Plus a walk-forward: for each season S, fit on <S, choose threshold on <S,
  apply to S, never revisit. No season is both selection and test.

2.5 THE BETTING RULE
  Qualify a spread when ALL THREE hold:
    |disagreement| >= 3.0
    |predicted movement| >= 0.5
    the two point the same way, i.e. (disagreement > 0) == (movement < 0)
  Bet the side the line is predicted to move toward, at the opener.

  Totals: |predicted total movement| >= 1.25 only. There is no working second
  signal for totals (tested, see 2.7).

2.6 RESULTS
  Movement model (walk-forward, opener + features vs opener-only control):
    select  corr 0.301   direction 60.0%   CLV +0.35
    holdout corr 0.327   direction 65.3%   CLV +0.66
    opener-only control: corr -0.025 / +0.064, direction 43.6% / 47.2%
    -> the football features, not mean reversion, drive it

  Qualifying record:
    holdout 2025     26-19   57.8%   95% CI [43.3, 72.2]
    walk-forward     30-23   56.6%   95% CI [43.3, 69.9]   ROI +8.1%
    totals holdout   30-23   56.6%   (movement >= 1.25, one signal only)

  Dual-signal decomposition (walk-forward, d>=7.0, m>=0.5):
    movement only       55.2%  (467 bets)
    disagreement only   58.4%  (185 bets)
    BOTH agree          59.5%  ( 74 bets)
    -> each layer improves the last; this justifies the rule's shape

  Margin prediction vs the market (walk-forward, n=1090):
    our model     corr 0.327   MAE 10.29
    market close  corr 0.455   MAE  9.51
    best blend    10% model / 90% market -> 0.457 vs market 0.455
    -> we do NOT beat the close; the model adds ~nothing on top of it

  ATS betting our disagreement with the CLOSE:
    |edge|>=0  51.6% | >=3  53.4% | >=6  54.5% | >=10  57.1% (n=57)
    -> monotone rising, the shape an edge would have, but tiny at the top

2.7 TWO EXECUTION FINDINGS THAT ARE STRONGER THAN THE MODEL ITSELF

  TIMING. CLV by how long a pick has been sitting:
    under 6 hrs   +1.55 CLV   70% positive   (n=106)
    6-24 hrs      +0.86       59%            (n= 76)
    1-3 days      +0.42       43%            (n=102)
    3+ days       -0.05       15%            (n= 95)
    Paired on the same game, early beats late by +1.46 points at t=7.0.
    This is by far the most statistically solid finding in the project.
    Acted on: the CLV logger now runs every 3 hours instead of daily.

  LINE SHOPPING. A better number than DraftKings exists on 78% of games, worth
    ~+0.30 points per bet across 2021-2025, using 9 liquid books.
    Acted on: the UI shows best line/book/price per side.

  CLV CONVERSION (does CLV actually become wins?):
    NFL, CLV >= 1 point earned -> 58.6% (944 bets)
    Gradient: 0.5-1 -> 50.0% | 1-2 -> 57.2% | 2+ -> 60.0%

2.8 IDEAS TESTED AND REJECTED (NFL)
  All have scripts in the repo. None is shipped.

  - Totals second signal. A proper total-POINTS model vs the opening total
    correlates with movement (+0.32 select / +0.19 holdout) but does not
    convert: alone it degrades from 52.8% to 49.1% as the threshold rises, and
    combined with movement it reads 60.3% on select and 48.1% on holdout.
  - Multi-book panel as a model feature. Improves movement correlation in both
    periods (0.277->0.299, 0.230->0.287) but the paired CLV gain is +0.05 to
    +0.09 at t=0.7-1.4. Not significant.
  - Key-number crossing as a selection filter. Never helps; on totals it hurts
    (holdout 51.7% crossing vs 62.5% not crossing).
  - Key-number RESISTANCE as a feature. The effect is real (lines opening on 3
    stay put 30% of the time vs 20-24% at 2.5/3.5) but it governs WHETHER a
    line moves, not WHICH WAY, so it is orthogonal to a directional bet.
  - Predicted-public reverse line movement. Built a public-lean proxy from
    favorite tilt and franchise popularity; it FAILED its own sanity check
    (correlation with subsequent movement +0.038, i.e. nothing), so the RLM
    hypothesis was not actually tested. Two explanations fit and the design
    cannot separate them: bad proxy, or public lean already priced into the
    opener.
  - Sharp-vs-retail divergence. Retail books (DK, FanDuel, MGM, Caesars,
    BetRivers) vs sharp books (Pinnacle, BetOnline, LowVig). The mechanism is
    real: they disagree on 42% of games and the line then moves toward the
    sharp number 57.4% / 58.7% / 64.9% as the gap widens. But as a model
    feature it does not convert.

2.9 EMPIRICALLY DERIVED KEY NUMBERS (2018-2025, share on N vs mean of N±1)
    margin  3   14.4% vs 4.9%   2.92x
    margin 10    4.9% vs 1.7%   2.85x
    margin 14    5.1% vs 1.8%   2.83x
    margin  7    8.5% vs 5.5%   1.55x
    total  51    4.1% vs 2.1%   1.93x
    total  44    4.0% vs 2.9%   1.39x
    total  41    3.5% vs 2.6%   1.33x
    total  47    3.2% vs 2.6%   1.21x  (not key)
    Note "3 and 7" undersells it: 10 and 14 spike as hard as 3.


================================================================================
PART 3 — COLLEGE FOOTBALL MODEL
================================================================================

3.1 DATA SOURCES
  CollegeFootballData API   games, per-game advanced stats, drives, SP+, FPI,
                            Elo, recruiting talent, returning production, venues
  The Odds API (NCAAF)      line snapshots, DraftKings anchor
  Sample: 3,791 FBS-vs-FBS games, 2020-2025

3.2 TEAM CROSSWALK
  136 FBS programs, mapped from BOTH the Odds API naming and CFBD naming to a
  single canonical key. Validated against 12 historical snapshots; 98 non-FBS
  entities correctly excluded. Traps handled explicitly: Miami vs Miami (OH),
  Ole Miss, Louisiana vs UL Monroe, "Youngstown St" (no period), inconsistent
  "University"/"College" suffixes, "San José State" (accented e), and four
  schools that renamed mid-sample (Sam Houston State, Dixie State/Utah Tech,
  Houston Baptist, Texas A&M-Commerce/East Texas A&M).

  FCS opponents (~100 games/yr) are excluded explicitly, not zero-filled.

3.3 FEATURES (~40)
  In-season form, opponent-adjusted where noted, rolled and SHIFTED so a game
  never sees itself:
    core (adjusted, L4 and L8):  off/def PPA, off/def success rate
    trench (L4):                 power success, stuff rate, line yards,
                                 second-level yards, open-field yards
    situational (L4):            standard-downs and passing-downs PPA / SR
    drives (L4):                 avg starting field position (off and def),
                                 drive scoring rate (off and def), drives count
  Season-level priors, all knowable before week 1:
    prior-season SP+ (overall/offense/defense)
    recruiting talent composite (current and prior year)
    returning production (percent PPA)
    prior-season FPI + offense/defense/SPECIAL TEAMS efficiencies
  In-season rating:
    weekly Elo, LAGGED one week (week W-1 used for a week-W game)
  Context:
    rest days, travel miles (haversine from each team's home venue),
    elevation change, dome, neutral site, conference game, games played

  All team features enter the model as HOME MINUS AWAY differentials.

3.4 KNOWN SAMPLING WEAKNESS — OPENER TIMING
  The CFB opener sits a median 147 hours before kickoff; the NFL's sits at 169.
  Worse, coverage is not uniform. Share of games whose opener snapshot falls on
  a Sunday (the earliest we capture):
    2020 1% | 2021 3% | 2022 10% | 2023 27% | 2024 67% | 2025 71%
  So the DEFINITION of "opener" drifts across the sample, and early training
  years carry systematically later openers than recent test years.
  OPEN QUESTION: how much does this invalidate the walk-forward?

3.5 RESULTS
  Margin prediction vs the market (walk-forward, n=2808):
    our model     corr 0.601   MAE 12.73
    market close  corr 0.657   MAE 12.10
    gap          -0.056        (NFL's gap is -0.128, so CFB is relatively closer)
    best blend    10% model / 90% market -> 0.657, same as market alone

  ATS betting our disagreement with the CLOSE:
    |edge|>=0  51.6% | >=3  52.9% | >=6  53.2%
    -> monotone rising for the first time after the latest feature revision

  Walk-forward with the NFL's dual rule:  39-43, 47.6%, CI [36.8, 58.4]

  Dual-signal decomposition (the important result):
                        d>=3.0                  d>=7.0
    movement only    52.0% (787)             52.0% (787)
    disagreement     53.8% (1619)            53.5% (635)
    BOTH agree       53.8% (327)             49.4% (160)
    -> In the NFL every layer improves the last. In CFB, requiring both signals
       to agree makes it WORSE at d>=7.0 (53.5% -> 49.4%) while cutting the
       sample from 635 to 160. The dual rule is inherited NFL structure that
       does not belong here.
    -> Disagreement-only is the best CFB rule at both bars, on much larger
       samples than anything else examined. NOT adopted: the 95% CI at d>=7.0
       is [49.7, 57.4] and contains break-even, and the bars were not chosen
       blind.

  Feature audit (ablation, drop-one-family, on margin prediction):
    dropping the new ratings (Elo + FPI)  costs -0.0334   <- most valuable
    dropping trench                       costs -0.0060
    dropping core EPA                     costs -0.0046
    dropping situational                  costs -0.0017
    dropping drives                       costs -0.0008
    dropping the old preseason family     HELPS +0.0064
    -> Elo/FPI have superseded SP+/talent/returning; they are substitutes and
       the newer ratings are better. The old family is retained only because it
       covers weeks 1-3 where Elo is stale by construction.
    Redundancy: L4 and L8 of the same metric correlate at a MEDIAN of 0.948.
    Two families (explosiveness, run/pass tendency) were removed because
    dropping them IMPROVED the model.

3.6 WHY COLLEGE IS HARDER DESPITE MORE DATA
  Residual SD of (margin + closing spread):  NFL 12.8   CFB 15.4
  Value of one point of line:                NFL 3.12pp CFB 2.58pp
  CLV conversion, CLV >= 1 point earned:     NFL 58.6% (944 bets)
                                             CFB 52.9% (2261 bets)
  College games are noisier and a point of line buys less win probability, so
  the same CLV lands barely past break-even instead of comfortably past it.


================================================================================
PART 4 — CROSS-CUTTING FINDINGS
================================================================================

4.1 THE CLV / WIN-RATE DISSOCIATION (the most troubling pattern)
  In BOTH sports, as the model's confidence in movement rises, CLV rises and
  win rate FALLS.
    NFL by |predicted movement|:  0.5-1.0 -> 68.0% win, +1.40 CLV
                                  1.0-1.5 -> 47.4% win, +1.97 CLV
                                  1.5+    -> 44.4% win, +2.39 CLV
  HYPOTHESIS (untested): large predicted moves track games where the line moves
  for REAL reasons — injury news, weather, late information. We get in early and
  capture the points, but the market moved because it learned something true,
  which means our early side was genuinely the worse one. If this is right, CLV
  is a worse proxy for edge than the whole project assumes.
  OPEN QUESTION: how would you test this cleanly?

4.2 EVERYTHING THAT WORKED WAS EXECUTION, NOT PREDICTION
  Shipped and paying: bet promptly (+1.55 CLV), shop the number (+0.30).
  Tested and rejected: six separate attempts to predict football better.
  Neither sport's model beats its own closing line.

4.3 BUGS FOUND (listed because they calibrate how much to trust the rest)
  - SIGN BUG. nflverse spread_line is POSITIVE when home is favored, the
    opposite of sportsbook convention. The target became correct + 2*spread,
    so the model learned to reconstruct the market line and was scored against
    a target containing that same term. Apparent 70-75% ATS was really 52.5%.
  - Playoff join fan-out: game_results holds 285 games/season, not 272, so
    joining predictions on (season, home, away) duplicated any pair that met
    again in January, showing one bet as both a win and a loss.
  - Lookahead in the timing analysis: picks are computed FROM the weekly
    opener, so snapshots before it are unusable. Including them showed a fat
    +1.94 CLV on the months-out lookahead board — pure look-ahead bias.
  - CFB postseason week numbers restart at 1, colliding with regular-season
    week 1; and CFBD labels Week 0 games as week 1. Together, 108 colliding
    keys that fanned an 8,984-row frame to 9,756.
  - Week-1 Elo leak: the fallback used the earliest AVAILABLE in-season rating,
    which is computed after week 1 was played. Fixing it reduced measured
    performance, confirming it had been inflating results.
  - An all-null feature (SP+ exposes offense.pace but never populates it) silently
    emptied the entire dataset via a dropna.
  - Multi-book merge on team pair without a season key fanned 1,612 games to
    4,656 and produced a fake 81.2% holdout.


================================================================================
PART 4B — RESULTS OF THE OUTSIDE CRITIQUE (added 2026-08-03)
================================================================================

An outside review of this document produced four checkable claims. All four were
tested. Results, in order of how much they changed things:

4B.1 A REAL BUG: THE WALK-FORWARD WAS NOT NESTED
  Thresholds were being chosen by scoring games the models had trained on
  (the selection set was a subset of the training set). Fixed with genuine
  out-of-fold selection. This changed the answer materially in both sports,
  so the previous walk-forward numbers were not trustworthy:
    NFL  30-23 (56.6%)  ->  36-29 (55.4%)
    CFB  39-43 (47.6%)  ->  39-25 (60.9%)

4B.2 THE SELECTION OBJECTIVE WAS WRONG FOR COLLEGE
  The selector maximised win rate with only a 12-bet floor, so it chose rules
  betting ~16 of ~700 college games a season — discarding the sample-size
  advantage that was the entire reason to model CFB. Replaced with the Wilson
  lower bound (rewards rate AND sample) and a candidate set that includes rule
  SHAPE, so a signal can be dropped rather than only tightened.
    NFL   36-29 (55.4%)  ->  120-86 (58.3%)  ROI +11.2%
    CFB   39-25 (60.9%)  ->  679-563 (54.7%) ROI  +4.4%
  Volume rose and the win rate rose WITH it, meaning the old high-bar
  selection was cutting good bets rather than concentrating an edge. Every
  fold now selects a low disagreement bar with NO movement-magnitude
  requirement, keeping only the direction-agreement condition.

  Clustered uncertainty (bootstrap over season-week blocks, since same-week
  bets share weather, news and correlated model error):
    NFL  49 blocks   binomial [51.5, 65.0]   cluster [52.4, 64.3]  CLEARS
    CFB  59 blocks   binomial [51.9, 57.4]   cluster [51.8, 57.7]  contains BE

4B.3 CLV IS NOT TOXIC — THE PREMISE SURVIVES
  Compared our bets against GENERIC bets earning the same CLV (back whichever
  side the line moved toward, every game, no model):
    NFL   ours 57.8% vs generic 56.5%   z = +0.28   no difference
    CFB   ours 56.3% vs generic 52.0%   z = +2.20   ours is BETTER
  The generic curve also validates CLV as a target: cover rate rises with
  realized CLV in both sports (NFL 50.5/52.0/62.6, CFB 47.9/50.1/55.8).
  The apparent dissociation was a conflation: REALIZED CLV converts normally,
  it is high PREDICTED MOVEMENT that underperforms. Those are different
  variables. This is why dropping the movement bar (4B.2) helped.

4B.4 PROBABILITY-WEIGHTED CLV — THEORY RIGHT, NO MEASURABLE GAIN
  Built an empirical cover curve conditioned on the closing line. A point is
  emphatically not uniform:
    across 3  (-3.5 -> -2.5)  +8.8pp     empty (-6.0 -> -5.0)  +4.0pp
    across 10 (-10.5 -> -9.5) +5.4pp     empty (-9.0 -> -8.0)  +2.7pp
    across 7  (-7.5 -> -6.5)  +4.3pp
  But it does not predict wins better:
    NFL  corr(points,won) +0.038   corr(prob,won) +0.006   worse
    CFB  corr(points,won) +0.102   corr(prob,won) +0.101   identical
  Kept as a reporting unit because it orders CFB buckets monotonically
  (51.8/52.5/56.6/59.9) where points wobble (52.8/55.4/54.4/58.4).

4B.5 THE MOST IMPORTANT RESULT — LABEL PERMUTATION
  Ran the ENTIRE pipeline 25 times per sport on data whose team-feature rows
  were shuffled within season, leaving lines/movement/margins correctly paired
  so only the model's ability to know anything is destroyed.

    NFL  real 58.3% (206 bets)
         permuted mean 49.2%  sd 3.4  min 39.5  max 55.2
         0/25 reached the real result    p <= 0.038    +2.7 sd

    CFB  real 54.7% (1242 bets)
         permuted mean 50.8%  sd 3.2  min 44.3  max 64.2
         1/25 reached the real result    p = 0.077     +1.2 sd

  The headline is the permuted SD of ~3.3 points. That is this pipeline's
  research-multiplicity tax: feature families, threshold sweeps, rule shapes
  and a per-fold selector can jointly manufacture about +-3.3pp of apparent
  edge from pure noise. A result needs to clear 50% by roughly 7 points
  before it means much.

  This RETIRES an earlier headline. The CFB 60.9% on 64 bets, reported during
  this session as the best result yet, is inside the noise envelope — one
  permutation of pure noise reached 64.2%. That rule should be treated as dead.

  Caveat: with 25 permutations the smallest achievable p is 1/26 = 0.038, so
  the NFL figure means "no permutation reached it", not a precise estimate.

4B.6 A CORRECTION MADE DURING THIS ROUND
  It was reported mid-session that 44% of NFL bets earned negative CLV and won
  58.9%, implying CLV was not the mechanism. That was wrong: the band lumped
  ZERO-CLV bets in with negative ones. Split properly:
    negative CLV  28-23  54.9%  CI [41.2, 68.6]
    zero CLV      25-14  64.1%  CI [49.0, 79.2]
    positive CLV  67-49  57.8%  CI [48.8, 66.7]
  Negative-CLV bets sit on break-even, and by season run 44.4/65.2/47.4 —
  carried entirely by 2024. There is no anomaly to explain.

CURRENT STANDING AFTER ALL OF THE ABOVE
  NFL  120-86 (58.3%), ROI +11.2%, cluster CI [52.4, 64.3], permutation
       p <= 0.038. The strongest position the project has held.
  CFB  679-563 (54.7%), ROI +4.4%, cluster CI [51.8, 57.7], permutation
       p = 0.077. Not established.
  NOTE: the LIVE app still uses |disagreement|>=3 AND |movement|>=0.5 AND
  agreement. The evidence now says the movement bar should be removed. That
  change has NOT been made.


================================================================================
PART 5 — WHAT WE MOST WANT CRITIQUED
================================================================================

1. Is CLV a valid optimization target? Section 4.1 shows CLV and win rate
   moving in OPPOSITE directions as model confidence rises, in both sports.
   That is the single most uncomfortable result here.

2. Is the NFL 56.6% real, or survivorship? It is 30-23 across four walk-forward
   seasons with a CI of [43.3, 69.9], and its per-season record is 50.0 / 41.7 /
   76.9 / 55.6. The 2024 fold (10-3) carries most of it.

3. Is the CFB disagreement-only result (53.8% on 1,619 bets) worth pursuing,
   or is it the next thing that will evaporate? It is the largest sample we
   have and above break-even at two thresholds, but the thresholds were not
   chosen blind.

4. The CFB opener definition drifts across the sample (Section 3.4). Does that
   invalidate the walk-forward, and is there a defensible way to correct it
   short of discarding 2020-2022?

5. Are we wrong to have abandoned outcome prediction? Neither model beats its
   close, but the CFB margin gap (-0.056) is half the NFL's (-0.128), and CFB
   ATS-vs-close is now monotone rising (51.6 / 52.9 / 53.2).

6. What obvious feature families are still missing? Known gaps: QB
   continuity/injury for CFB (transfer portal, starter changes), weather for
   CFB, coaching changes, havoc rate and points-per-opportunity (available only
   as leaky season aggregates), and anything about market microstructure beyond
   what is listed in 2.8.

7. Sample size. NFL yields ~45 qualifying bets a season. Is there a defensible
   statistical approach to this problem that we are not using — Bayesian
   shrinkage, hierarchical pooling across sports, something else?
