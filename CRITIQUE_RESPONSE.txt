You reviewed our NFL/CFB betting methodology and made ~20 recommendations. This

================================================================================
1. YOU FOUND A REAL BUG (your section 7)
================================================================================

You flagged that our "nested" walk-forward threshold selection might not be
nested. It was not:

    tr  = df[df.season < S]                        # models trained here
    sel = df[(df.season < S) & (df.season >= S-2)]  # a SUBSET of tr

Thresholds were chosen by scoring games the models had trained on — in-sample
optimisation wearing a nested costume.

Fixed with genuine out-of-fold selection: each selection season s is predicted
by a model trained only on seasons < s. This changed the answer materially in
BOTH sports, so our previously reported walk-forward numbers were not
trustworthy:

    NFL   30-23 (56.6%)  ->  36-29 (55.4%)
    CFB   39-43 (47.6%)  ->  39-25 (60.9%)

The CFB swing is large and counterintuitive — the leak was making CFB look
WORSE. In-sample fit flatters extreme cases, so the leaky selector kept
choosing a high disagreement bar (d>=7.0) that generalised badly.

2. POINT BY POINT ON YOUR TEN PRIORITY RECOMMENDATIONS
================================================================================

(1) REPLACE RAW POINT CLV WITH PROBABILITY-AWARE CLV — DONE, THEORY CONFIRMED,
    NO MEASURABLE GAIN

Built an empirical cover curve conditioned on the closing line (not a normal
approximation, which would smooth away the key-number spikes that are the whole
point). Your premise is correct — a point is emphatically not uniform:

    across 3   (-3.5 -> -2.5)   +8.8pp
    across 10  (-10.5 -> -9.5)  +5.4pp
    across 7   (-7.5 -> -6.5)   +4.3pp
    off 3      (-3.0 -> -2.5)   +4.4pp
    empty      (-6.0 -> -5.0)   +4.0pp
    empty      (-9.0 -> -8.0)   +2.7pp

Crossing 3 is worth more than three times crossing nothing. But converting CLV
to probability did NOT improve it as a predictor of whether a bet wins:

    NFL   corr(points, won) +0.0382    corr(prob, won) +0.0062   WORSE
    CFB   corr(points, won) +0.1018    corr(prob, won) +0.1014   identical

Kept as the reporting unit because it orders CFB buckets monotonically
(51.8/52.5/56.6/59.9) where point buckets wobble (52.8/55.4/54.4/58.4).

We also tested your specific proposed mechanism — that big moves happen on big
spreads where points are cheap. That is NOT the mechanism. Mean |spread| is flat
across movement bins (4.56/5.05/5.10/4.88, corr -0.024). The real compositional
difference is KEY-NUMBER PROXIMITY: 67% of low-movement games sit within half a
point of 3 or 7, against 36% of high-movement games. Low-movement bets earn
fewer points but in the expensive part of the distribution.

(2) GRADE AGAINST A CONSENSUS/SHARP CLOSE — NOT DONE
We have multi-book quotes at the weekly OPENER for 2020-2025 (9 books), but not
at the close, so a historical consensus-close benchmark cannot be built. Live
capture of 9 books every 15 minutes started this week, so this becomes possible
for 2026 forward. Agreed it is a real gap.

(3) SEPARATE MOVEMENT OCCURRENCE / DIRECTION / MAGNITUDE (hurdle model) —
    NOT DONE
We think this is your best untested idea, specifically because it explains a
result we had already filed as a dead end: our key-number RESISTANCE feature
predicts whether a line moves but not which way, and a single regression target
compresses those. We had concluded "resistance is orthogonal to a directional
bet"; your framing is that it was being asked to solve the wrong subproblem.

(4) TEST WHETHER HIGH-CONFIDENCE MOVES ARE ADVERSELY SELECTED — DONE. CLV IS
    NOT TOXIC.

You called this the single most decisive next analysis. We agree, and it came
back clean. Comparing our bets against GENERIC bets earning the same CLV
(generic = back whichever side the line moved toward, every game, no model):

                    generic   ours     gap
    NFL  CLV 0.5-1   50.5%    47.6%
         CLV 1-2     52.0%    55.8%
         CLV 2+      62.6%    63.5%
         POOLED      56.5%    57.8%   +1.3   z = +0.28
    CFB  CLV 0.5-1   47.9%    55.4%
         CLV 1-2     50.1%    54.4%
         CLV 2+      55.8%    58.4%
         POOLED      52.0%    56.3%   +4.3   z = +2.20

Toxic-CLV hypothesis rejected in both sports. In college our CLV is
SIGNIFICANTLY BETTER quality than generic CLV. The generic curve also validates
CLV as a target: cover rate rises with realized CLV in both sports.

This also resolved the CLV/win-rate dissociation, and the resolution is subtler
than either of our hypotheses. REALIZED CLV converts to wins normally. It is
high PREDICTED MOVEMENT that underperforms. Those are different variables and
we had been conflating them.

(5) STANDARDISE CFB BY FIXED TIME-TO-KICKOFF HORIZONS — PARTIALLY DONE
Diagnosed the problem quantitatively before spending anything: our CFB opener
sat a median 129 hours before kickoff against the NFL's 169. Added Sunday
snapshots (the board posts Saturday night), moving it to 147h. Movement captured
rose from 1.42 to 1.54 pts and the dataset grew 3,574 -> 3,791 games.

The drift you identified is worse than we realised and is structural: the share
of games whose opener falls on a Sunday runs 1% / 3% / 10% / 27% / 67% / 71%
across 2020-2025. The Odds API simply did not carry early-week CFB lines in the
early years. So the prediction task genuinely changes across the sample and we
cannot fix it retroactively. Your fixed-horizon proposal is the right answer for
2026 forward; for history, the common-support restriction would cost us most of
2020-2022.

(6) KEEP OUTCOME PREDICTION, USE CALIBRATED COVER PROBABILITY — NOT DONE
We did measure the raw comparison (walk-forward):
    NFL  ours 0.327  market 0.455  gap -0.128  best blend 10% model
    CFB  ours 0.601  market 0.657  gap -0.047  best blend 10-15% model
CFB is relatively closer to its market than the NFL is to its own. Neither beats
the close. We have not built the gating model you propose.

(7) FREEZE PROSPECTIVE 2026 ARMS — NOT DONE. ~5 weeks to Week 1.

(8) CLUSTERED UNCERTAINTY — DONE
Cluster bootstrap over (season, week) blocks, 3000 resamples:
    NFL  49 blocks   binomial [51.5, 65.0]   cluster [52.4, 64.3]
    CFB  59 blocks   binomial [51.9, 57.4]   cluster [51.8, 57.7]
Interesting side finding: clustering barely moved the NFL interval but widened
CFB's, i.e. college bets are more correlated within a week than NFL bets.

(9) QB STATE / PERSONNEL CONTINUITY / MICROSTRUCTURE FEATURES — NOT DONE
We added prior-season SP+, FPI (incl. special teams), recruiting talent,
returning production and weekly Elo to CFB, which are strength ratings rather
than the personnel-state features you describe. No QB continuity model.

(10) TURN THE PRODUCT TOWARD QUOTE VALUATION — NOT DONE

ALSO DONE, from your section 17: LABEL PERMUTATION. See section 4 — this turned
out to be the most important thing in your review.

3. SOMETHING WE CHANGED THAT YOU DID NOT SUGGEST, AND IT MATTERED MOST
================================================================================

Our walk-forward selector maximised out-of-fold WIN RATE with only a 12-bet
floor. For the NFL that is defensible since volume is capped. For college it was
actively counterproductive: it selected rules betting ~16 of ~700 games a
season, discarding the sample-size advantage that was the entire strategic
reason to model CFB.

Replaced with the Wilson LOWER BOUND (rewards rate AND sample; z=1.0, since at
1.96 only the largest samples ever win), and widened the candidate set to
include rule SHAPE — both-agree / disagreement-only / movement-only — so the
selector can drop a signal rather than only tighten a bar.

    NFL   36-29 (55.4%)  ->  120-86 (58.3%)   ROI +11.2%
    CFB   39-25 (60.9%)  ->  679-563 (54.7%)  ROI  +4.4%

Volume rose AND the win rate rose with it, which means the old high-bar
selection was cutting good bets rather than concentrating an edge. Every fold
now selects a low disagreement bar with NO movement-magnitude requirement,
keeping only direction agreement.

That converged with finding (4) from opposite directions: the movement model's
MAGNITUDE is useful for predicting movement and bad for choosing bets. We have
since removed the movement-magnitude bar from the live NFL spread rule.

4. THE LABEL PERMUTATION TEST — THE MOST DECISIVE RESULT
================================================================================

Ran the ENTIRE pipeline (nested out-of-fold selection, rule-shape choice,
evidence-weighted objective, walk-forward grading) 25 times per sport on data
whose TEAM FEATURE rows were shuffled within season. Lines, movement and margins
stay correctly paired, so market structure and base rates survive and only the
model's ability to know anything is destroyed.

    NFL spreads   real 58.3%   permuted mean 49.2%  sd 3.4  max 55.2
                  0/25 reached it    p <= 0.038    +2.7 sd

    CFB spreads   real 54.7%   permuted mean 50.8%  sd 3.2  max 64.2
                  1/25 reached it    p = 0.077     +1.2 sd

    NFL totals    real 59.5%   permuted mean 52.6%  sd 5.9  max 63.8
                  5/25 reached it    p = 0.231     +1.2 sd

The number we consider most valuable in the entire review process is the
PERMUTED SD of roughly 3.3 points for spreads. That is our pipeline's
research-multiplicity tax, measured rather than argued about. A result needs to
clear 50% by about 7 points before it means much.

Two consequences:

  * It RETIRED one of our own headline claims. The CFB 60.9% on 64 bets, which
    we had reported as the best result of the day, is INSIDE the noise envelope
    — one permutation of pure noise reached 64.2%.

  * The NFL totals noise floor is 52.6-52.8%, ABOVE the 52.38% break-even. A
    permuted totals pipeline is profitable on average, because the opener
    survives as a feature (mean reversion) and a period under-bias persists
    through shuffling. Totals must clear ~52.7%, not 52.38%, before anything is
    demonstrated. We had not appreciated this.

Caveat: with 25 permutations the smallest achievable p is 1/26 = 0.038, so the
NFL figure means "noise never reached it", not a precise estimate.

5. CORRECTIONS WE HAD TO MAKE TO OUR OWN CLAIMS
================================================================================

  * We reported that 44% of NFL bets earned NEGATIVE CLV and won 58.9%, and
    concluded CLV was not the mechanism. WRONG on both counts — the band lumped
    ZERO-CLV bets in with negative ones. Split properly: negative 28-23 (54.9%,
    CI [41.2, 68.6]), zero 25-14 (64.1%), positive 67-49 (57.8%). By season the
    negative-CLV bets run 44.4/65.2/47.4, carried entirely by 2024. No anomaly.

  * We claimed "the dual-signal rule is NFL furniture that does not belong in
    CFB". Too strong. It hurts at HIGH disagreement bars (d>=7: 53.5% -> 49.4%);
    at low bars it is fine, and the corrected walk-forward selects a dual rule
    at d>=2.0 in every fold.

  * We spent much of a session arguing CFB was measurably worse than the NFL.
    That case rested on the leaky number you caught.

6. TOTALS — A GAP WE ONLY FOUND BECAUSE OF THIS PROCESS
================================================================================

Everything above was done on SPREADS. Our live totals rule (|predicted movement|
>= 1.25, single signal) came from an earlier select/holdout sweep and had never
been walk-forwarded, nested or permutation-tested.

Parity audit first, since a fair comparison requires equal development:
  FEATURES  TOTAL_FEATURES is a strict SUPERSET of SPREAD_FEATURES — all 57
            plus dome, temperature, wind, precipitation. Nothing was applied to
            spreads and withheld from totals.
  HARNESS   identical.
  CAVEAT    our first totals run was too GENEROUS — it supplied a total-points
            disagreement signal that production does not have.

Both formulations tested:
    with disagreement (not in production)  80-59  57.6%  p=0.154  +1.3 sd
    ACTUAL live rule (movement-only)      128-87  59.5%  p=0.231  +1.2 sd

The live totals rule has the best point estimate of anything we tested and its
binomial CI clears break-even — and noise reaches it one time in five. Adding
the second signal made it worse (59.5% -> 57.6%), independently confirming an
earlier finding that the totals disagreement signal does not convert.

8. WHAT WE ARE STUCK ON — WHERE WE WANT YOUR VIEW
================================================================================

1. Is the NFL spread result now strong enough to bet, or is 25 permutations and
   206 walk-forward bets still too thin? What would you require?

2. The totals noise floor being ABOVE break-even (52.6-52.8%) surprised us. Is
   that a genuine property of totals markets in this era, an artefact of keeping
   the opener as a model feature, or a sign our permutation scheme is wrong for
   totals specifically?

3. We now have a measured "research multiplicity tax" of ~3.3pp for spreads and
   ~5.9pp for totals. Is there a principled way to USE that number — e.g. as a
   prior, a required margin, or a shrinkage factor — rather than just as a
   sanity check?

4. Our selector demonstrably failed once: in the totals run it chose the
   two-signal rule out-of-fold and the simpler rule beat it in test. Given that,
   how much should we trust the same selector's choice on spreads, which is now
   live?

5. CFB opener drift is structural (Sunday coverage 1% -> 71% across 2020-2025)
   and cannot be fixed retroactively. Restrict to common support and lose most
   of 2020-2022, model horizon explicitly, or accept the drift and weight?

6. Should we stop adding and freeze? We are ~5 weeks from NFL Week 1. Our
   instinct is to freeze the NFL spread rule, drop the "qualifying" flag from
   totals (visible and tracked, not recommended), keep CFB out of the app
   entirely, and let 2026 produce one clean prospective record. Is that the
   right call, or is there something you would insist on testing first?

7. Given (4) in section 2 showed our CLV is of normal-to-better quality, and the
   permutation shows the spread rule survives noise — what is the strongest
   remaining argument that this whole thing is still a mirage?
